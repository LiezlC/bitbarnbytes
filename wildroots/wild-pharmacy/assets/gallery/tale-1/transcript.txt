# The Door of Bitter Waters and the Mycelium Login

The blue-light glare of the BitSoil SageByte mainframe recedes into a dull ache behind your eyes. You step away from the terminal and feel the floor transition from hard plastic to cool, damp loam. The air is thick with the scent of wet earth. Hedges of glitch-mycelium rise around you in the dark. They are woven with RGB-fiber vines that cast a toxic green light against the loam browns of the path. A heavy scent of thujone drifts from the shifting shadows. A massive silhouette moves at the edge of your vision: the heavy presence of the Minotaur waiting for a logic-error. Hexi the hedge-witch appears through the mycelium as if she were part of the garden itself. Her voice is dry and warm. You have the look of the digitally shipwrecked, she says. You look like a soul who has forgotten the taste of the ground while chasing the blue-light glare.

· · ·

The Gate of Six Doors stands before you in the teal-flickering gloom. It is a terminal login screen built from dead USB sticks and gnarled roots. To pass, you must solve the riddle of the kougoed. Two plants sit on a stone plinth in the flickering light. One is the true Kanna, the Sceletium shepherds chew to find their smile. The other is a toxic look-alike, a counterfeit of the mainframe world that personifies the Minotaur's trap. You look for the specific succulent leaves of the true Sceletium. You remember the stories of the plant that makes one happy. You choose the Door of Bitter Waters and the gate pulses with a mono-green glow. As you pass through the threshold, a flicker of glitch-code scrapes your arm. A Bulbine plant grows near the base of the gate. Snap the leaf, Hexi whispers. Press the fresh gel onto the skin. The cooling succulent jelly pulls the sting of the glitch away.

· · ·

The path leads you into a room of impossible angles and magenta-edged shadows. Neon wireframes rise from a floor of composted circuit boards. This is the stair-stack, an Escher room rebuilt as a data structure. A small figure stands by the bridge. He is covered in stray feathers and looks toward the high wireframes with a worried face. He speaks the rule of the grove in a quiet voice. Take the leaf and spare the bark, he says. You must never harvest what you cannot name. He hands you a small bundle of Buchu leaves. You steep the leaves in a cup of water until the scent of the Cape mountain apothecary fills the room. You read the steeped leaves as the steam rises, catching a wry herb-fortune in the patterns. You drink the infusion and feel a sharp clarity. The remedy restores your focus and prepares your mind for the path ahead.

Toward the center of the labyrinth, a green-pixel steam rises from a great cauldron. A thirteen-hour clock glitches on the wall as it moves toward the final hour. At the base of the cauldron, a cracked blue-light phone is breaking down into the soil. It is composting into medicine for the digitally damned. You walk deeper into the heart of the maze where the wild things grow deep.
