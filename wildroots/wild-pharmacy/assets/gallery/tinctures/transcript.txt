Botanical extraction captures nature's medicine, but fresh herbs,
wilts, and lose their potency in days.
To trap those healing elements before they rot,
you submerge a fresh sprig of rosemary in high-proof alcohol.
Down at the microscopic level, that alcohol acts as an aggressive
physical solvent. It actively attacks the plant,
stripping away and breaking down its rigid outer cell walls.
As those walls rupture, the plant's active medicinal compounds
and essential oils spill out, and dissolve directly into the surrounding liquid.
Boiling water breaks open these cells too, like when you make tea,
but water breeds bacteria, causing it to quickly spoil.
But high-proof alcohol is completely hostile to any microbes.
It catches the released compounds and suspends them in a sterile environment
where degradation physically stops.
This exact mechanism is extraction, separating the stable medicinal payload
from the highly perishable physical structure.
After a few weeks, that dead depleted rosemary sprig
is simply strained out of the jar and tossed into the compost.
What's left behind is a concentrated golden medicine,
the pure healing power of the plant, effectively frozen in time.
