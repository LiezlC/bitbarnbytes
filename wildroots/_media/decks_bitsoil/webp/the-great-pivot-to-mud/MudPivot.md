ACT I — THE USER

01 · The Problem: Terminal Feed-Fatigue

[NARRATOR — intimate, dry; wonder underneath the weariness]

Meet Brayden. Vice President of Engagement. He holds forty thousand strangers' attention. He cannot remember the last time he noticed rain.

His phone glows in the mud like a small, demanding moon. Somewhere behind his eyes, a loading wheel turns.

He has monetised his mornings. His evenings are pending approval.

Even his exhaustion has excellent reach.

02 · Target Demographic: Terminal Feed-Fatigue

[NARRATOR — a smile on the satire; soften at the end]

The algorithm knows Brayden's fears and his midnight shopping. It calls this knowing him.

His grandmother knew him by the way he came through the door.

He searches for premium silence. The first result offers a free trial, followed by monthly emptiness.

For once, something in him refuses to click.

03 · Disrupting the Detox

[NARRATOR — briefly glossy, like an advertisement discovering its conscience]

Then comes BitSoil. A farm gleaming in the wreckage of a sales pitch. Mud, but make it tech.

The brochure promises transformation. They all do. This one has a goat chewing the guarantee.

Brayden books a place.

He thinks he's buying an escape. Outside, a world waits to welcome him back.

04 · Off-Grid. On-Brand.

[NARRATOR — widen into the landscape; give the exchange room]

At the gate, the silos wear their circuitry like borrowed jewellery. Chickens patrol a sunset nobody has colour-corrected. The signal drops to ten percent.

BRAYDEN: Ten? How do the goats even post?

CHICKEN: They don't. Terrible personal brands. Lovely goats.

NARRATOR: He holds his phone higher. The sky declines to improve its service.

All this space, and nothing asking him to fill it. He feels the first small terror of an afternoon that belongs to him.

ACT II — THE GARDENER

05 · Zero-Step Optimization

[NARRATOR — curious; SAGEBYTE welcoming rather than guru-like]

Among the dead monitors, a woman with a pitchfork surveys the wreckage of several futures.

SAGEBYTE: Welcome. Leave your performance indicators by the gate.

BRAYDEN: Are they safe there?

SAGEBYTE: Oh, no. The weather will get them.

NARRATOR: Her boots are muddy. Her hands are busy. She appears, alarmingly, to have no intention of becoming a thought leader.

06 · Chief Glitch Gardener

[SAGEBYTE — grounded, amused, with a little gravel in the warmth]

I'm SageByte. Chief Glitch Gardener. I mend what's useful and let the rest become a lesson.

That motherboard? Still working. That promise of endless growth? Lovely under the tomatoes.

We compost confusion here. Turn it over. Let air in. Give the difficult bits time.

You're welcome to help. You don't have to arrive improved.

07 · We're Not Being Managed

[NARRATOR — relish the goat's entrance; GOAT cheerfully authoritative]

A goat emerges from the wreckage, wearing the grin of a hostile takeover with excellent digestion.

GOAT: We're not being managed. We're being a farm.

NARRATOR: It chews a lanyard. Head of Strategic Connections. Finally, a networking event with nutritional value.

Brayden starts to ask who's in charge. Then notices a full water trough, a mended gate, a little room in the shade.

ACT III — ONBOARDING

08 · Agile Hardware Management

[NARRATOR — mock institutional grandeur, then affectionate observation]

The org chart has three goats. Structured Data stands in its box. Chaos Algorithm has eaten its box. The Disruptor is horizontal.

Brayden recognises senior management immediately.

Yet watch them a little longer. One finds a foothold. One tests the fence. One knows when the heat requires a lie-down.

Three ways of meeting the same afternoon. No goat is sent on a course to become the other goat.

09 · Agile Compliance

[NARRATOR — conversational; PIG slow and deliciously certain]

Beside a glowing prohibition sign, a pig lowers itself into the earth with devotional precision.

BRAYDEN: Is that allowed?

PIG: Highly recommended.

NARRATOR: The pig has considered the policy. The policy has failed to consider the pig.

SageByte watches the water spread around its shoulders. A useful rule protects something living. This one seems chiefly concerned with protecting the sign.

10 · Burn the Forms

[NARRATOR — quicken through the paperwork; leave the last line bare]

Brayden has brought forms. A consent form. A compliance form. A form confirming that he understands the other forms.

The pig studies this airborne bureaucracy, then sits down in the middle of it.

A wet, authoritative silence.

Somewhere, a committee feels a disturbance.

SageByte passes him a towel without asking him to prove he's wet.

11 · Multi-Speed Biological Processing

[NARRATOR — steady with the pony, a brief flicker of speed for the husky]

The pony moves at the speed of a pony. The husky moves at the speed of a thought you should probably write down.

Both arrive. Occasionally in different fields.

Brayden was taught to call every slower rhythm a bottleneck. Here, the slow rhythm carries weight. The quick one finds a way around.

SAGEBYTE: Watch what each can do. You'll spend less time apologising for what they can't.

12 · Zero Strategy. Maximum Velocity.

[NARRATOR — delight, with a clean deadpan landing]

Two dogs detonate through the tidy hedge. Leaves fly. The strategic framework becomes a smell.

Brayden opens his mouth to request a roadmap.

By then, joy has crossed the field.

There will be repairs. Someone will fetch a rake. But for one glorious second, nobody mistakes the immaculate hedge for the purpose of being alive.

13 · Real-Time Notification Protocol

[GOAT — one bright interruption; NARRATOR lets the air settle afterwards]

GOAT: I'm HERE!

NARRATOR: A goat arrives through Brayden's calendar. Nine o'clock scatters. Ten o'clock loses a corner. The quick sync is, at last, brief.

His reflex: dismiss. Remind me later.

This notification has warm breath and inconvenient hooves. It wants company.

For years, he has been available to everyone. The goat is asking whether he might actually be present.

14 · Loud. Present. Unapologetic.

[NARRATOR — buoyant; let the final thought land without solemnity]

The goat boards a drone with the confidence of someone entirely uninsured.

It has no content strategy. It has a view.

Across the sky goes one magnificent bleat: badly targeted, impossible to monetise, felt in the ribs.

Brayden laughs before he can decide whether it's worth sharing.

The sound surprises him. His own laugh, unedited. He had forgotten it could leave his body without first becoming an asset.

15 · Subjective Scale Augmentation

[NARRATOR — playful spectacle; HORSE dry, without contempt]

A puppy throws a wolf-shaped shadow over the afternoon. Tiny paws. Monumental projection. A pitch deck with teeth.

HORSE: That's a lot of wolf for one puppy.

NARRATOR: The puppy keeps glowing. Let it. A small creature sometimes needs a large imagining to cross the yard.

The trouble begins when the imagining demands everyone else's dinner.

16 · Blind Ambition Meets Total Apathy

[NARRATOR — lively terrier energy against the horse's stillness]

The terrier pitches. The horse chews.

Market domination. Chew. Exponential scale. Chew. A once-in-a-generation opportunity to revolutionise sticks.

HORSE: There's one behind you.

NARRATOR: The terrier looks. There is.

For a moment, ambition meets an actual stick. An awkward encounter. It hadn't budgeted for satisfaction.

The horse waits patiently. There's room in this field for wanting things, and room for having enough. Even a visionary needs somewhere to stop.

17 · Apathy at Scale

[NARRATOR — fond, tired humour; a long, comfortable HORSE vowel]

The horse stands inside the spreadsheet. A whole warm animal, ankle-deep in somebody's theory.

HORSE: Eh. Seems fine.

NARRATOR: Every quarter, Brayden must announce an unprecedented quarter. Even the word unprecedented looks tired.

Under the grid, grass keeps growing. No forecast. Just the green insistence of another mouthful.

ACT V — THE FENCES

18 · Cross-Functional Permeability

[NARRATOR — constricted at first; release with the fence]

Brayden stands in a cube labelled Hard Boundaries. Outside, the husky and the horse have found a design flaw: friendship.

SAGEBYTE: A fence can keep you safe. It can also get very pleased with itself.

NARRATOR: She lifts the latch. Such a small movement, after all that architecture. Air passes between the posts. He can almost feel it reach a room inside him that has been shut for years.

19 · Fences Don't Make Kings

[NARRATOR — warm mischief; savour the space after “kingdom”]

The husky sails through the glowing lines, delighted to discover they have no substance.

Brayden has spent years guarding a rectangle on a chart. He called it his kingdom. It came with no windows and seventeen recurring meetings.

On the other side of the fence, the dog rolls onto its back.

No conquest. No succession crisis. Just the astonishing luxury of a belly in the sun.

20 · The Diagnostics of Chaos

[NARRATOR — crisp contrast; PIG a comfortably sceptical consultant]

The pig presents a dashboard. Brayden brightens. Finally, something that resembles a problem he can bill for.

On one side: ownership, obedience, straight lines. On the other: muddy edges, shared shade, try it and see.

PIG: We do keep track. Water. Food. Who's limping. Useful things.

NARRATOR: Brayden looks again. The farm has plenty of attention. Very little of it is devoted to proving that it is paying attention.

21 · Data Liquidity

[NARRATOR — amused wonder; PIG almost luxuriantly unhurried]

The growth chart melts into a pink pool. The pig gets in. At last, a liquid asset with practical applications.

BRAYDEN: But how do you know you're succeeding?

PIG: Come back tomorrow. See who's thirsty.

NARRATOR: He considers this disappointing lack of abstraction.

A graph can show the rain. It cannot get wet for you. He takes off one shoe, then the other. The earth accepts this modest contribution to the discussion.

ACT VI — THE PIVOT

22 · Attention Is a Metric. Corn Is a Meal.

[NARRATOR — slow the contrast; no sermon, just the two hands]

Two hands. One reaches towards a red alert that will breed another red alert. One holds corn.

The first keeps asking. The second opens.

SAGEBYTE: Watch the birds. They know when they've eaten.

NARRATOR: Brayden watches grain disappear into small, purposeful lives. Such a simple transaction. Something needed; something given. No captive audience. No retention strategy. The hens are free to leave satisfied.

He tries to imagine presenting that business model. Then he feeds another hen.

23 · Zero-Emission Algorithms

[NARRATOR — lightly satirical through the machinery; tender at the seed]

Toxic attention goes in. Chaos does something expensive-looking. A golden seed comes out.

Zero emissions, declares the heading. The pig raises an eyebrow.

But look at that seed: a future small enough to hold, stubborn enough to split its casing. Brayden has attended launches with less ambition and considerably more catering.

24 · Don't Brand the Myth

[NARRATOR — hushed delight; the instruction firm and simple]

A hoof lands on the keyboard. The green letters flicker: don't brand the myth.

Brayden is already imagining the merchandise.

Then the hoof presses again.

Leave one beautiful thing without a purchase button. Let a story pass from hand to hand, picking up fingerprints. Let it belong to the people who need it, without first making them customers.

25 · Pivot to Mud

[NARRATOR — a quick, startled turn, then genuine vulnerability]

Down comes the mallet. Down goes the portal. Brayden's carefully curated elsewhere meets the ground.

BRAYDEN: But everything's in there.

NARRATOR: His work. His friends. The proofs he exists.

SageByte stays beside him. Even a ridiculous cage can be frightening to leave when you've furnished it with your whole life.

26 · Pivot to Mud (the takeover)

[NARRATOR — let the choice unfold; CHICKEN clipped and devastating]

Then he kneels. His own hands enter the mud. The screen disappears beneath a brown, unmarketable thumbprint.

BRAYDEN: Is this mindfulness?

CHICKEN: It's mud. Give it a minute before you launch the podcast.

NARRATOR: He laughs. A little shakily.

Around his fingers, the earth is cool and particular. A blade of grass bends against his wrist. The small moon goes dark; the afternoon goes on shining. He had been waiting for a better version of himself to begin living. His ordinary hands seem perfectly able to begin.

ACT VII — THE MUD

27 · Slip. Fall. Repeat.

[NARRATOR — comic momentum; keep the reassurance matter-of-fact]

A bulldog surfs on the human resources manual. Finally, the policy supports someone.

It slips. Falls. Gets up muddy, with no statement for the press.

Brayden waits for the embarrassment to arrive.

Nobody sends it.

A fall here is a thing that happened. It doesn't have to become your name.

28 · Welcome to the Mud

[NARRATOR — joyful, generous, brisk]

In they come: hooves, paws, ears, glorious miscalculations. Pink tyres. Flying turf. The entire farm abandoning its good side.

There will be no usable group photograph.

Brayden is splashed before he can arrange his face. Someone laughs; someone makes room. For once, belonging happens faster than his explanation of why he deserves it.

29 · If You Show Up, You Fit

[NARRATOR — tender without becoming precious; GOAT casual]

Only his face remains above the mud. Around him, little grinning creatures appear to find this entirely acceptable.

A goat offers a clipboard, then eats the eligibility criteria.

GOAT: Saves time.

NARRATOR: He has entered so many rooms rehearsing a useful version of himself. The clever one. The easy one. The one who doesn't need much.

Here, his wet, bewildered face is enough to start with. Someone nudges closer. No introduction required.

30 · Listen to the Soil. Literally.

[NARRATOR — lower the volume; leave a breath after “listens”]

He brings gauges. Mud density. Slop viscosity. He's still Brayden.

Then he puts his ear to the earth and listens.

A small rustle. His own breathing. The world continuing below the range of his importance.

No answer. For once, that doesn't feel like being ignored.

ACT VIII — THE DROP

31 · 122 BPM of Pure Disobedience

[NARRATOR — rhythmic, delighted; leave the last beats to the music]

Then the hoof finds the drum.

One hundred and twenty-two beats a minute, and nobody asks whether dancing aligns with the mission.

The pig is already moving. Compliance has become percussion.

Brayden's shoulders remember something his calendar forgot. A body has its own reasons for being here.

For a few bright bars, he lets it explain.

32 · We Mind the Grid

[NARRATOR — expansive; PIG quietly pleased with itself]

From above, the grid loosens into spirals. The husky makes a whirlpool. The pigs contribute horizontal expertise.

PIG: We mind the grid. It gets lonely otherwise.

NARRATOR: Lines still cross the field. They no longer get the final word. Life keeps spilling over, making those untidy connections on which everything depends.

ACT IX — THE ZERO STEP

33 · Zero-Step Optimization (the barn wall)

[NARRATOR — let the corporate words rattle; become still underneath]

On the barn wall, arrows chase their tails. Synergise. Monetise. Ideate. Hustle. Pivot. A whole illuminated theology of never arriving.

Below it, Brayden sits in the hay. A goat leans against his knee.

Nothing here would survive a board presentation.

The goat is warm. The light moves slowly across the barn, requiring no assistance. He doesn't volunteer any.

34 · Zero-Step Optimization Achieved

[NARRATOR — quiet amusement, unforced]

The arrows scatter. The chickens peck. The husky has taken its ambitions onto the roof for a nap.

Brayden lies in the straw. The moment can finish happening before he extracts the lesson.

Tomorrow there will be work. Buckets, repairs, a creature needing breakfast.

Tonight, the world can carry its own weight for a while. He lets it.

35 · We're Just Being… Soon.

[NARRATOR — cosy, slightly drowsy; HORSE almost asleep]

A horse, a goat, a puppy and a laptop settle into a heap. At last, the machine has some sensible colleagues.

BRAYDEN: What happens next?

HORSE: Soon.

NARRATOR: It's a poor deadline and an excellent answer.

Breathing rises and falls through the pile. Nobody outranks the need for warmth. Even the laptop has stopped insisting that something urgent is available to install.

ACT X — THE HARVEST

36 · Downgrading the Portal to a Pebble

[NARRATOR — intimate, with the smile gently returning]

Later, he lifts the battered phone. Sunset rests on its glass. Beyond it, more sunset than any screen could keep.

Such a clever little object. Such a large job he'd given it: tell me who I am; tell me whether I'm loved; keep the dark away.

Now it sits in his palm, quiet as a pebble.

He raises his eyes. The light has reached the faces of his companions. He nearly missed that.

37 · Confusion Has Roots

[NARRATOR — wonder, with no mystical grandstanding]

In the story's soil, the old circuitry branches into roots. A seedling lifts its green question through the broken screen.

Imagine our cleverness learning to stay for the consequences. Tools we can mend. Power gathered from daylight. Water held for the dry months. Knowledge passed along with the spade.

The future might arrive like this: something useful growing from a mistake we finally stopped defending.

38 · Cancel the Detox. Feed the Goat.

[NARRATOR — smile through the ordinary practical detail; SAGEBYTE gentle]

Brayden holds the bottle. The small goat drinks with its whole, ridiculous heart.

He had come to recover himself. Now somebody needs him to keep the bottle at the right angle.

SAGEBYTE: There you go.

NARRATOR: No revelation ceremony. No certificate. Just milk, warm breath, the soft push of a living forehead.

For this minute, he knows exactly what his hands are for. It is a great relief.

39 · Life Is Better Together

[SAGEBYTE — directly to one listener; warm, unhurried, no sales-pitch uplift]

Come as you are. Tired counts. Unsure counts. Bring the clever bits, the broken bits, the joke that still makes you laugh on a difficult day.

There's work here, and room to rest. Something to mend. Someone to feed. Enough sky to look up into together.

Life is better together.

Come on. Let's walk.

And bring a coat. Hope is lovely, but the evening gets cold.

Fin. Grown in the BitSoil. One portal retired. Several forms eaten. The goat has declined to become a thought leader.
